---
title: "Design decisions"
description: "The choices that were not obvious, and why."
---

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The on-device model is a bonus, not the product" icon="cpu">
    Extraction and plain-language rewriting fall back to a reviewed glossary and deterministic
    parsing whenever the model will not load — which on a cheap handset is most of the time. The
    fallback is what ships and what gets demonstrated.

    A system that works on the phones people actually own is a better story than inference that
    needs 450MB downloaded first.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="MedRekk never supplies a reference range" icon="flask">
    A lab result is compared only against the interval printed on that same report. If none is
    printed, it says so and stops.

    Reference intervals vary by laboratory, assay, age, sex, pregnancy and altitude — a
    haemoglobin range from a Lagos lab does not apply in Jos, which is 1,200m higher. A built-in
    table would quietly turn every one of those differences into a false "below range" on
    somebody's phone, and a false reassurance in the other direction.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A detected signal is never a diagnosis" icon="triangle-alert">
    Out of range is common and often unimportant; in range rules nothing out. Every association is
    phrased as *can be associated with*, never *you have*, and the suggested next step is the same
    sentence for every flagged result — because distinguishing routine from urgent is a clinical
    judgement, and getting it wrong in the reassuring direction is as harmful as the frightening
    one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The printed emergency card carries two facts" icon="printer">
    Blood group and severe allergies. Everything else stays behind the QR.

    Printing the whole card defeats the QR: nobody scans a square when the answer is written
    beside it, and both things the scan exists for are lost — the record staying **current**, and
    every look being **written down**. A card printed in January listing no allergies is worse
    than no card once one is added in March.

    A wallet is also lost far more often than it is used in an emergency, and a list of conditions
    in a stranger's hands is the most sensitive and least urgent part of a record.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A patient may delete what a clinician wrote" icon="trash-2">
    If the record belongs to the patient, it belongs to them including the parts they disagree
    with. Everything the visit produced goes with it, and any allergy among them comes off the
    emergency card.

    Soft-deleted, so the trail can record that something was removed on a date without recording
    what. That is what keeps the claim honest.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The image never leaves the phone" icon="camera">
    Only a SHA-256 is sent. A clinician who later doubts a document can be shown the patient's own
    copy and check it is byte-for-byte the one attached — without MedRekk ever holding it.

    The file is kept in `documentDirectory`, not the cache the OS reclaims. It was in the cache
    once, which meant attachments were being deleted by the system: a record claiming a document
    behind it that could not produce one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dev auth is refused, not discouraged" icon="lock">
    `AUTH_MODE` defaults to `jwt` and `dev` is rejected outright in production and whenever
    `DATABASE_URL` is set.

    It defaulted to `dev` once. A host that never set the variable served an open record store —
    which is exactly what a default must never do.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
