## One app, two roles

The signed-in account's role decides which app you get. A patient sees their record, emergency
card, access control and history; a health worker sees patient lookup, visit capture and the
on-device model. Dual-role accounts are supported — a doctor is also somebody's patient.

## Capture

A patient or worker can add to a record three ways, and all three produce the same thing: a
**document**, kept in full, with whatever was extracted from it listed underneath.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Type" icon="keyboard">Up to 5,000 characters. Paste a whole report</Card>
  <Card title="Photograph" icon="camera">ML Kit reads printed text. The image stays on the phone</Card>
  <Card title="Speak" icon="mic">Dictated and transcribed on the device</Card>

<Info>
Only a SHA-256 of a photographed document is sent. The image never leaves the handset — so a
clinician who later doubts a document can be shown the patient's own copy and check it is
byte-for-byte the one attached, without MedRekk ever holding it.
</Info>

## Plain language

Clinical shorthand is decoded rather than defined:

```
Tab Ciprotab 500mg BD x 4/7
→ Ciprofloxacin (written here as Ciprotab) — an antibiotic used for urinary,
  gut and some other bacterial infections. Take 500mg, twice a day, for 4 days.
```

`BD` is twice a day, `x 4/7` is four days, `x 2/52` is two weeks, `PRN` is only when needed.
None of that is taught outside the profession.

<Note>
This runs on a reviewed glossary and deterministic parsing, not a model. It works on a phone with
nothing downloaded, which is most phones. The on-device model is an enhancement when it loads.
</Note>

## Design system

`components/design/split.tsx` — dark chrome carrying identity and state, a bright sheet of
content lifted over it, a floating tab bar whose open tab rises rather than lights up. Colour
comes only from `tokens.ts`.
