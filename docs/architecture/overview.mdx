## The pieces

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The app — the patient's copy" icon="mobile">
    Holds the record in SQLite and reads from it first. The network is an enhancement: a phone
    with no signal can still show the whole record, every document in full, and the most recent
    60 entries of the audit trail.

    Health workers use the same app for the other side of the flow — looking a patient up,
    requesting access, and recording a visit by typing, dictating or photographing it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The backend — consent and the trail" icon="server">
    Owns the parts that cannot live on a handset: who is allowed to see what, for how long, and
    what was actually looked at. Express 5 and TypeScript, Zod at every boundary, Postgres in
    production and an in-memory implementation behind the same interfaces for tests.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The web frontend — everyone without the app" icon="globe">
    A clinician reads an approved record in a browser with no account. A responder looks up an
    emergency card from a scanned QR. An administrator approves health-worker credentials. Next.js
    16, App Router, server components unless state demands otherwise.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Three ways into a record

| Route | Who uses it | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The app | Patients, health workers | An install; works offline after first sign-in |
| A browser | A clinician with an approved session | The link the patient approved. No account |
| A phone keypad | A patient with no smartphone | A USSD code and a PIN. No app, no internet |

The emergency card sits outside all three: it is readable from a QR by anyone, without approval,
because the person it describes may be unable to give any.
